# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Crypto traders and curious newcomers on Stellar testnet: hackathon judges, Stellar community members, and memecoin degens used to pump.fun. They browse a list of coins, open one, and buy or sell within seconds, usually on a laptop or a phone.

## Product Purpose
The retail channel for Stellar's tokenized assets. A memecoin launchpad where every coin's bonding-curve reserve is a yield-bearing sovereign bond from Etherfuse (CETES and Tesouro; US Treasury only on mainnet, since it does not work on testnet) instead of XLM: every meme launched buys sovereign debt, and a quarter of every fee goes to the meme's vault. Half of the vault is paid to the meme's holders as dividends in the bond, by balance; the other half buys the meme back and burns it after graduation. Robinhood Chain showed the pattern: memecoins are how retail ends up holding tokenized assets. The app has to feel as fast and familiar as pump.fun: discover coins, see live activity, trade, launch your own. It also has to show what makes Hooks different: the bond behind each coin, what its reserve earns per day, how much sovereign debt memes have bought in total, and what each holder can claim on the Dividends page. Success means a 3-minute demo with no friction: log in with email, get a bond, create, buy, watch a coin graduate to Soroswap and its vault buy back, then claim the dividends that every trade paid.

## Brand Personality
The Hooks Daily: a tabloid in the vein of the Daily Bugle. Ink on newsprint, one press red, Anton shouting the headlines, Newsreader telling the story, Franklin setting kickers, bylines and the market tables in agate. Every meme is a news story with a headline written from its numbers, a byline (its creator) and a full-color photo about its name (the lead image of the Wikipedia article its name finds, until creators can upload their own; the ticker in a red box when there is none); the front page has ears, a flag, an edition line and one lead story. Rules separate content; only ads get a box (the trade coupon, the classifieds form, the subscription card). Charts alone keep market green and red.

Avoid the props of fake vintage: coffee stains, torn edges, sepia, paper textures, typewriter fonts. The newspaper comes from the system (rules, hierarchy, copy, credits), not decoration.

## Anti-references
- A finance terminal or a SaaS dashboard: too serious, no memecoin energy in the structure.
- Generic crypto landing pages: hero copy and stats with no tokens to look at.
- Anything that hides the trade action or makes users read before they can act.

## Design Principles
1. Tokens first: the first screen is coins and live activity, not explanations.
2. Earned familiarity: copy proven launchpad patterns (pump.fun) so traders know where everything is.
3. Show the backing: every coin surfaces its RWA pair, reserve and yield next to price and market cap.
4. Act in one place: the trade panel is always visible on the coin page.
5. Identity is cheap: every wallet and coin gets a recognizable avatar and name with zero setup.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG AA contrast, keyboard-reachable controls, visible focus, reduced-motion fallbacks for the live feed. Buy and sell are not distinguished by color alone (labels and signs too).
